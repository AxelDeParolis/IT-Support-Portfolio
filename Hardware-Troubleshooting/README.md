Hardware Troubleshooting – Desktop Computer Power Failure

Project Overview

This project simulates a Level 1 IT Support incident involving a desktop computer that fails to power on.

The objective was to demonstrate systematic hardware troubleshooting, power supply diagnosis, motherboard inspection, fault isolation, and incident documentation.

Note: This is a simulated exercise for learning and portfolio development. No physical hardware repairs or component replacements were performed.

Environment

Device: Desktop Computer

Operating System: Windows 10/11 (simulated)

Support Level: Level 1 IT Support

Ticket ID: INC-003

User: Michael Brown

Department: Sales

Priority: High

1. Incident Description

The user reported that the desktop computer would not power on.

When pressing the power button:

No indicator lights appeared.

No fans started spinning.

The monitor remained blank.

The computer showed no signs of receiving power.

2. Initial Troubleshooting

The first step was to investigate the external power supply path.

The following checks were performed in the simulated scenario:

Verified that the power cable was securely connected.

Confirmed that the wall outlet was working.

Checked that the PSU switch was turned ON.

Confirmed that the computer still showed no signs of power.

Key Concept: Start with the simplest and least invasive troubleshooting steps before considering hardware replacement.

3. Power Supply Unit (PSU) Investigation

After the initial checks, the investigation focused on the Power Supply Unit (PSU).

An approved PSU tester was used in the simulated scenario.

Result: The PSU was functioning correctly.

This helped narrow down the possible cause of the problem.

Safety Consideration: Power supply units should never be opened for internal inspection. They can retain dangerous electrical charges even when disconnected from power.

4. Motherboard and Power Connection Investigation

Since the PSU was functioning correctly, the next step was to investigate the motherboard and its power connections.

The investigation identified a disconnected front-panel power switch connector.

The connector links the computer case's power button to the motherboard.

Root Cause: The front-panel power switch connector was disconnected from the motherboard.

5. Resolution

The technician reconnected the front-panel power switch connector correctly.

After the corrective action:

The computer powered on successfully.

The system completed its startup process.

Windows loaded successfully.

The user was asked to verify that the computer was functioning normally.

6. Ticket Documentation

The simulated incident record included:

Issue: Desktop computer would not power on.

Initial Checks: Power cable, wall outlet and PSU switch verified.

PSU Diagnosis: PSU tested and confirmed functional.

Root Cause: Disconnected front-panel power switch connector.

Corrective Action: Connector reattached correctly.

Verification: Computer powered on and Windows loaded successfully.

Status: Resolved.

Key Learning Outcome

This exercise demonstrated the importance of following a systematic troubleshooting methodology when diagnosing desktop hardware failures.

It reinforced the need to check simple causes first, isolate faulty components through appropriate testing, follow electrical safety procedures, verify successful resolution, and document corrective actions accurately.
